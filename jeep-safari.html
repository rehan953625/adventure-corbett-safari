The Classic 4x4 Gypsy Experience

Why Open Jeep Safari is the Best Way to Explore Corbett

An open 4x4 Maruti Gypsy safari is the most adventurous and intimate way to explore the rugged forest trails, ancient Sal canopies, seasonal riverbeds (sots), and savannah grasslands of Corbett Tiger Reserve. Accompanied by a forest-authorized local driver and a certified forest guide, you have the flexibility to stop, listen to alarm calls of deer and langurs, and track fresh pugmarks.

Each Gypsy accommodates a maximum of 6 adult passengers plus 2 children below 5 years, ensuring 360-degree unobstructed views for wildlife photographers and nature lovers. Entry is regulated by strict zone quotas to protect the tiger ecosystem, and vehicles must exit the gate strictly before sunset.

🚙 Vehicle Specifications 4x4 High-clearance Gypsy with open roof-rail seating, custom padded seats, and certified green emission norms.
⏱️ Safari Duration Approx. 3.5 to 4 hours per shift (Morning: ~6:00 AM to 9:30 AM | Afternoon: ~1:30 PM to 5:30 PM).
Corbett Tourism Sectors

Where Do Jeep Safaris Go? (Safari Zones & Entry Gates)

Jim Corbett Tiger Reserve is segmented into multiple distinct tourism zones, each with its dedicated entry gate, unique habitat, and specific operating season.

1. Bijrani Safari Zone

Second only to Dhikala in natural beauty, Bijrani is renowned for its sweeping Sal forests, expansive Chaurs (grasslands), and dramatic seasonal riverbeds, offering some of the highest tiger sighting probabilities among day safari zones. Accessed through the Amdanda Gate (just 1 km from Ramnagar town), Bijrani remains open from 15th October to 30th June and stays closed during the monsoon. Visitors exploring this zone frequently spot Royal Bengal Tigers, Asian Elephants, Sloth Bears, Chital, and Sambars.

2. Jhirna Safari Zone

Located on the southern boundary of Corbett Tiger Reserve, Jhirna features dry deciduous scrub, secondary bamboo patches, and wide stream crossings (sots) that provide clear visibility for wildlife viewing. Entered via the Dhela / Khara Gate (around 16 km from Ramnagar), Jhirna is highly accessible and stays open round the year subject to weather and forest road conditions. Key wildlife includes Wild Asian Elephants, Sloth Bears, Royal Bengal Tigers, and Great Hornbills.

3. Dhela Safari Zone

Opened in 2014, Dhela is a rich eco-tourism zone characterized by mixed Sal and Rohini flora, quiet waterholes, open grasslands, and dedicated watchtowers ideal for calm birding and predator tracking. Accessed from the Dhela Gate (approximately 15 km from Ramnagar), the zone remains open throughout the year subject to rain safety notices, supporting Leopards, Tigers, Nilgai, Barking Deer, and more than 300 resident and migratory bird species.

4. Garjiya / Ringora Safari Zone

Situated between Bijrani and Dhikala along the scenic Kosi river belt, the Garjiya (Ringora) zone boasts undulating hilly terrain, thick Sal canopies, and thrilling forest trails with frequent big-cat movement. Jeep safaris enter via the Ringora / Garjiya Gate (about 10 km from Ramnagar) during its operating season from 15th November to 30th June, with regular sightings of Tigers, Wild Boars, Sambars, Red Jungle Fowls, and Himalayan raptors.

5. Phato Safari Zone

A rapidly popular eco-tourism sector in the western Terai forest belt, Phato is known for its dense Sal woodlands, historic water channels, a unique forest treehouse watchtower, and peaceful wilderness trails away from heavy crowds. Entered through the Phato Gate (~22 km from Ramnagar), this zone remains open for most of the year and is inhabited by Tigers, Leopards, Asian Elephants, Spotted Deer, and Jackals.

6. Durga Devi Safari Zone

Set in the northeastern hilly terrain of the reserve, Durga Devi offers steep Shivalik mountain vistas along the confluence of the Ramganga and Mandal rivers, famous for Golden Mahseer fish and rich avian life. Accessed via the Durga Devi Gate near Mohan (about 28 km from Ramnagar), the zone operates from 15th November to 15th June and is a prime habitat for Royal Bengal Tigers, Leopards, Wild Elephants, Otters, and Crested Serpent Eagles.

7. Sitabani Buffer Zone

Located outside the core tiger reserve boundary under the Ramnagar Forest Division, the Sitabani landscape features tranquil Sal and bamboo forests, shallow river streams, and the ancient Valmiki and Sitabani heritage temples. Entered via the Teda Gate (approximately 5 km from Ramnagar), Sitabani remains open all year round and is popular for serene forest drives, birdwatching, and spotting Elephants, Leopards, Barking Deer, Sambar, and Kingfishers.

8. Sonanadi Safari Zone

Spread across the scenic northwestern buffer of Corbett Tiger Reserve in the Pauri Garhwal hills, the Sonanadi Wildlife Sanctuary zone is famous for its dense Sal and Sheesham forests, rugged Shivalik valleys, and the confluence of the Palain and Mandal rivers with the Ramganga reservoir. Please note that the entry gate for Sonanadi Zone (Vatanvasa / Halduparao Gate) is accessed from the Kotdwar side rather than Ramnagar. Operating from 15th November to 15th June, this tranquil zone is an important elephant corridor and supports healthy populations of Wild Asian Elephants, Royal Bengal Tigers, Leopards, Sambars, Sloth Bears, and over 550 bird species.

9. Pakhro Safari Zone

Located within the Sonanadi Eco-Tourism Range of Corbett Tiger Reserve, Pakhro is a lush, less-crowded forest sector known for its thick Bhabar-Terai Sal canopy, perennial woodland streams, and rich floral diversity. Just like Sonanadi, the entry gate for Pakhro Safari Zone (Pakhro Gate) is located on the Kotdwar side (easily accessible for travelers arriving via Kotdwar / Lansdowne route). Known for peaceful wilderness tracking away from heavy tourist rush, Pakhro offers sightings of Asian Elephants, Tigers, Leopards, Barking Deer, Wild Boars, and diverse Himalayan foothills birdlife.

*Note on Dhikala Zone: Day Jeep Safaris are not permitted inside Dhikala core zone for visitors staying outside the forest. Day visitors explore Dhikala via Canter Safari, whereas Jeep Safari in Dhikala is exclusively reserved for visitors holding confirmed overnight Dhikala Forest Rest House (FRH) permits.

Operating Slots

Jim Corbett Jeep Safari Timings & Reporting Rules

Safaris operate in two fixed daily shifts: Morning and Afternoon. Reporting at the gate 30 minutes in advance with original photo IDs is mandatory.

🌅 Morning Shift (Sunrise Safari)

  • Winter (October to February): 6:00 AM / 6:30 AM to 9:30 AM / 10:00 AM
  • Summer (March to June): 5:30 AM / 6:00 AM to 9:00 AM / 9:30 AM
  • Why Choose: Crisp jungle atmosphere, fresh night tracks/pugmarks, morning territorial roaring, and brilliant bird activity.

🌇 Afternoon Shift (Sunset Safari)

  • Winter (October to February): 1:30 PM / 2:00 PM to 5:00 PM / 5:30 PM
  • Summer (March to June): 2:30 PM / 3:00 PM to 6:00 PM / 6:30 PM
  • Why Choose: High probability of predators lounging near Ramganga, Kosi, and inland waterholes during warmer afternoon hours.

📋 ID Verification & Entry Rules

  • Mandatory Original ID: Carry the exact government photo ID used during permit reservation.
  • Non-Transferable: Permits cannot be swapped or exchanged with another person once generated by the forest portal.
  • Gate Reporting: Gypsy reaches your Ramnagar resort 30–45 minutes before gate opening time.

How to Book Your Jim Corbett Jeep Safari (Step-by-Step)

Corbett safari permits are released in limited daily quotas. Follow these 4 easy steps to secure your Gypsy safari smoothly.

Step 1: Choose Date & Shift

Decide whether you wish to explore in the morning or afternoon shift. For weekend travel, plan at least 30 to 60 days in advance.

Step 2: Select Preferred Zone

Pick your desired zone (Bijrani for grassland & tigers, Jhirna/Dhela for all-season wildlife, Garjiya for scenic river trails, or Phato for dense Sal).

Step 3: Provide Traveler IDs

Share names, ages, gender, and government ID numbers of all members (max 6 adults per Jeep).

Step 4: Permit Issuance & Pickup

Permit is generated through the forest system. On safari day, the authorized 4x4 Gypsy arrives at your resort for pickup.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Jim Corbett Jeep Safari

Everything you need to know about pricing, guide fees, permits, and zone selection.

1. Is the forest guide fee included in the Jeep Safari package cost?
No, the guide fee is NOT included. As per the regulations of the Uttarakhand Forest Department, certified registered nature guides are allotted on a rotational roster directly at the entry gate. Clients are required to pay the guide fee (approx. ₹700 to ₹1,000 per safari shift) directly in cash to the guide at the gate.
2. How many people can sit in one Jeep for safari?
Each authorized 4x4 Maruti Gypsy can seat a maximum of 6 adult visitors plus 2 children aged below 5 years. Along with visitors, 1 certified driver and 1 registered forest guide occupy the front seats.
3. Which safari zone has the highest chance of tiger sighting?
While wildlife sightings are entirely natural and cannot be guaranteed anywhere in the wild, Bijrani Zone and Dhikala Zone are historically famous for tiger sightings. Among all-year-round zones, Jhirna, Dhela, Garjiya, and Phato consistently report excellent sightings of elephants, sloth bears, deer, and tigers.
4. What documents do I need to bring for the Jeep Safari?
Every visitor must carry their original government-approved photo ID (Passport, Voter ID, or approved photo ID) whose details were entered during the permit booking. Entry will be denied by forest guards if the original ID does not match the permit.
5. Can we cancel or reschedule our Jeep Safari permit?
Under Uttarakhand Forest Department rules, official safari entry permits are strictly non-refundable and non-transferable once issued. Dates, shifts, names, and zones cannot be altered once booked on the government system.
