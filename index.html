WELCOME TO THE CORBETT REGION

Welcome to Jim Corbett

Jim Corbett National Park, in Uttarakhand, is one of India’s best-known natural destinations. Its varied landscapes include sal forests, grasslands, river corridors and the foothills of the Himalayas. These habitats support a rich range of wildlife and birdlife, making the wider Corbett region appealing to nature lovers, photographers and travellers who want to experience the outdoors.

A visit to Corbett can be more than a single activity. Depending on the season, availability and your interests, you may plan a permitted forest safari, explore nearby villages and riverside landscapes, enjoy local food, or choose accommodation around the park landscape. Each experience offers a different way to appreciate the region, from early-morning forest atmosphere to relaxed evenings after a day outdoors.

Stretching across the Nainital and Pauri Garhwal districts with Ramnagar serving as the main administrative gateway, the reserve features dramatic terrain shifts—from the wide Patli Dun valley and Kosi riverbeds to undulating Shiwalik ridges and broad savannah-like grasslands locally known as chaurs. Exploring these ecosystems with an authorised forest guide introduces visitors to track signs, alarm calls, seasonal flora and conservation history.

Wildlife sightings are never guaranteed: animals move freely in their natural habitat, and every safari is different. A responsible visit means following park rules, listening to authorised staff, keeping a respectful distance from wildlife, avoiding litter and keeping noise to a minimum. Safari zones, timings, permits and access rules can change, so confirm current details with the relevant official authority before travelling.

Key Habitats & Wildlife Highlights

  • Mammals: Natural habitat of the Royal Bengal Tiger, Asian Elephant, Leopard, Spotted Deer (Chital), Sambar, Hog Deer, Barking Deer, Wild Boar and Sloth Bear.
  • Aquatic Life: Ramganga reservoir and river channels support Gharial, Mugger Crocodile, Smooth-coated Otter and Golden Mahseer.
  • Avian Diversity: Over 550+ recorded species of resident and migratory birds including Great Hornbill, Crested Serpent Eagle, Pallas’s Fish Eagle and Red Junglefowl.
  • Flora: Dominated by Sal (Shorea robusta), Haldu, Rohini, Kusum, Bamboo brakes and flowering Flame of the Forest (Palash) in spring.

TERRAIN • ENTRY GATES • ACCESSIBILITY

Jim Corbett Safari Zones Explained

Jim Corbett is divided into distinct tourism zones to manage visitor movement and protect wildlife habitats. Each zone has its own entry gate, forest character and seasonal schedule.

Dhikala Zone

Core Zone • Canter / FRH
Entry: Dhangarhi Gate (~18 km from Ramnagar) Open: Mid-Nov to June

The largest and best-known landscape inside the reserve, overlooking the scenic Patli Dun valley, Ramganga river channels and expansive grasslands (chaurs). Daytime visitors without an inside Forest Rest House reservation explore Dhikala via the official 16-seater Canter Safari.

  • Broad riverbeds, Ramganga reservoir views and large elephant herds
  • Daytime access via morning and afternoon Canter departures
  • Closed during monsoon months due to rising river levels

Bijrani Zone

Core Zone • Day Jeep
Entry: Amdanda Gate (~2 km from Ramnagar) Open: Mid-Oct to June

Located close to Ramnagar town, Bijrani combines thick Sal forests, open grasslands and seasonal stream beds (sots). Its varied terrain and proximity to town make it one of the most requested zones for daytime private Jeep Safaris.

  • Balanced mix of dense forest canopy and open grazing meadows
  • Convenient morning and afternoon Jeep Safari reporting point
  • Rich sightings of spotted deer, sambar, peacocks and raptors

Jhirna Zone

Open Year-Round*
Entry: Dhela / Jhirna Road (~16 km from Ramnagar) Mode: Private Jeep

Situated along the southern boundary of the park, Jhirna features dry deciduous secondary forest, wide rocky streambeds (rao) and open scrubland regenerated after historical village relocation. It offers excellent visibility and remains open across seasons subject to weather.

  • Known for sloth bear, wild elephant, sambar and nilgai habitats
  • Accessible during monsoon months unless heavy rain halts road entry
  • Great cliff viewpoints and open dry river crossings

Dhela Zone

Open Year-Round*
Entry: Dhela Gate (~13 km from Ramnagar) Mode: Private Jeep

Added as a dedicated eco-tourism zone in the southern buffer-core corridor, Dhela blends Sal, Rohini, Haldu and mixed vegetation with open grasslands and restored water bodies. It is prized by birdwatchers and wildlife photographers throughout the year.

  • High density of resident and winter-migratory bird species
  • Open throughout the year for morning and afternoon Jeep Safaris
  • Smooth forest tracks suitable for families and senior travellers

Durga Devi Zone

Hilly Terrain • Birding
Entry: Durga Devi Gate (~28 km from Ramnagar) Open: Mid-Nov to June

Set on the northeastern hilly boundary along the Ramganga and Mandal rivers, Durga Devi offers rugged Shiwalik slopes, cool mountain streams and dense forest cover. It is a favourite for quieter nature drives and spotting hill-forest bird species.

  • Scenic river confluences and Golden Mahseer breeding waters
  • Ideal for serious birding, landscape photography and quiet trails
  • Elevated forest drives with Himalayan foothill views

Garjia, Sitabani & Phato Zones

Scenic & Buffer Tracks
Entry: Ringora / Teda / Mohan Gates Mode: Private Jeep

Garjia (Girija) offers lush Sal forest tracks near the Kosi belt, while Sitabani and Phato reside in adjoining reserve forest divisions. These zones provide valuable alternatives when core permits are full or when travellers want a flexible buffer-forest experience.

  • Garjia features dense Sal cover and convenient access from Dhikuli resorts
  • Sitabani combines forest trails, ancient temple heritage and birding
  • Phato offers dense forest tracks and treehouse/Eco-stay proximity

SEASONAL PLANNING GUIDE

Best Time to Visit Jim Corbett

Each season transforms the Corbett landscape—from golden winter grasslands to warm summer waterholes and lush monsoon greenery.

November to February

Winter & Peak Birding Season

Pleasant sunny afternoons and chilly mornings (5°C to 25°C) make winter ideal for comfortable sightseeing and all-zone exploration once Dhikala and Bijrani open for the season.

  • Core zones (Dhikala, Bijrani, Durga Devi) open from mid-November
  • Peak arrival of migratory waterfowl and Himalayan foothill birds
  • Heavy warm layers required for early-morning open-top drives
March to Mid-June

Summer & Wildlife Spotting Season

As temperatures rise (20°C to 40°C) and dense undergrowth dries out, visibility across forest tracks increases and animals frequently gather near the Ramganga, Kosi and forest waterholes.

  • High wildlife movement around rivers, streams and shaded pools
  • Red silk-cotton (Semal) and Flame of the Forest bloom in spring
  • Early morning shifts are cool and refreshing; pack cotton clothes
Late June to October

Monsoon & Lush Green Season

Seasonal rains rejuvenate the Shiwalik hills (22°C to 32°C). While core zones close to allow forest regeneration and because riverbeds flood, southern buffer/eco-zones remain open subject to weather.

  • Jhirna, Dhela, Sitabani and Phato operate subject to daily road status
  • Dramatic mist-covered hills, flowing waterfalls and quiet resorts
  • Ideal for relaxed nature stays, photography and peaceful getaways

RESPONSIBLE WILDLIFE TOURISM

Important Park Rules & Visitor Checklist

Jim Corbett is a protected tiger reserve. Every visitor is required to follow official forest department rules for safety and habitat conservation.

Mandatory Permit & Entry Regulations

  • Original Government Photo ID: Every visitor listed on the permit must present the original ID (Aadhaar Card, Passport, Driving Licence or Voter ID) at the entry gate check-post.
  • Non-Transferable Permits: Safari permits are issued for a specific date, shift, zone and visitor list; names or dates cannot be swapped at the gate.
  • Compulsory Nature Guide: Entry into national park safari zones requires an official registered forest guide alongside the authorised safari driver.
  • Strict Entry & Exit Timings: Vehicles must enter and exit the forest within the official shift window; late arrivals may forfeit entry without refund.
  • Weather & Administrative Closures: The forest authority reserves the right to restrict or cancel zone access at short notice due to heavy rain, river flooding or wildlife management needs.

Forest Etiquette: Do's & Don'ts

  • Wear Earth-Toned Clothing: Choose muted shades such as olive green, khaki, brown, beige or grey so you blend quietly into the natural surroundings.
  • Stay Inside the Vehicle: Stepping down from the Jeep or Canter inside the safari zone is strictly prohibited except at designated fenced rest points.
  • Maintain Silence & Patience: Keep phones on silent, avoid loud conversations or music, and allow your guide to listen for natural forest alarm calls.
  • Zero Litter & No Feeding: Do not carry disposable plastic waste into the forest, never feed wild animals or birds, and never smoke or light fires inside the reserve.
  • Respect Wildlife Right of Way: Never pressure the driver to chase, crowd or block an animal’s path, and avoid using camera flash.

TRAVEL & LOCAL SIGHTSEEING

How to Reach Jim Corbett & Nearby Places

Ramnagar (District Nainital, Uttarakhand) is the main town and entry hub for Jim Corbett National Park, well connected by road and rail from northern India.

By Road (From Delhi NCR & UP/UK)

Ramnagar is approximately 250–260 km from New Delhi (around 5.5 to 6.5 hours by car) via Hapur, Gajraula, Moradabad bypass, Kashipur and Ramnagar. Regular state transport and private taxis also connect Nainital (~65 km), Haldwani (~55 km), Dehradun (~230 km) and Lucknow.

By Train (Ramnagar Railway Station)

Ramnagar Railway Station (Code: RMR) is located right in town, close to Amdanda Gate and the Canter boarding point. Direct trains operate from Old Delhi / Anand Vihar (including Ranikhet Express and Corbett Link Express), Moradabad, Kashipur and Haridwar.

By Air (Nearest Airports)

Pantnagar Airport (PGH) is the closest domestic airport at roughly 80 km (~2 hours by road). Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun (DED) is about 215 km away, while Indira Gandhi International Airport, New Delhi (DEL) (~260 km) offers the widest domestic and international flight connectivity.

Popular Sightseeing Places Around the Corbett Region

Garjia Devi Temple

Located near Dhikuli (~14 km from Ramnagar), this revered shrine sits atop a massive rock formation in the middle of the Kosi River and attracts visitors year-round.

Corbett Waterfall

Situated about 25 km from Ramnagar on the Kaladhungi highway, this scenic 20-metre waterfall is surrounded by peaceful teak and sal forests with a short nature walk.

Corbett Museum (Kaladhungi)

Housed in the heritage winter bungalow of Edward Jim Corbett at Choti Haldwani, showcasing archival photographs, personal letters, manuscripts and conservation history.

Kosi River & Sitabani Belt

Enjoy riverside walks along boulder-strewn Kosi banks, birdwatching trails in the Sitabani reserve forest and scenic drives toward Marchula and Kaladhungi.

COMMON VISITOR QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Find clear answers to common questions about safari permits, vehicle choices, pricing and trip preparation.

1. How far in advance should I enquire about a Jim Corbett safari?

Core zone permits (especially for weekend dates, holidays and peak months between November and June) are limited and often fill up several weeks in advance. We recommend sending your enquiry as early as possible with your travel dates and ID details so you can check real-time zone availability.

2. What is the difference between a Jeep Safari and a Canter Safari?

A Jeep Safari uses a private 4WD open vehicle accommodating up to 6 passengers, operating in zones such as Bijrani, Jhirna, Dhela, Durga Devi, Garjia and Sitabani. A Canter Safari uses a shared 16-seater open-top bus operating specifically into the Dhikala Zone from Ramnagar / Dhangarhi Gate, ideal for daytime visitors who do not have an overnight stay inside Dhikala.

3. Are tiger or wildlife sightings guaranteed on a safari?

No. Jim Corbett is a vast natural forest reserve where animals move freely across thousands of hectares. While guides and drivers use their experience to track pugmarks and alarm calls, sightings depend entirely on nature and luck. Every drive offers a rich wilderness experience of forest landscapes, flora and birdlife.

4. What documents are mandatory at the safari entry gate?

Every guest must carry the exact same original government-issued photo ID (Aadhaar Card, Passport, Voter ID or Driving Licence) whose number was used while issuing the entry permit. Foreign nationals must carry their original Passport.

5. What is included in the indicative prices shown on this website?

Prices shown on this page are indicative reference rates (for example, ₹7,500 per Jeep, ₹2,500 per Indian person for Canter, and ₹4,000 per Elephant outside the forest). Compulsory guide fees, specific zone permit variations, taxes or resort pickup charges beyond standard points may be payable separately—always confirm the exact written inclusion breakdown before paying.

6. Which safari zones remain open during the monsoon season (July to September)?

Core zones like Dhikala, Bijrani and Durga Devi close during the monsoon due to heavy rainfall and river flooding. However, southern and buffer zones such as Jhirna, Dhela, Sitabani and Phato generally remain open year-round, subject to daily weather conditions and forest department road safety clearance.

7. Is a Jim Corbett safari suitable for children and senior citizens?

Yes, families with children and senior citizens regularly enjoy safaris in Jim Corbett. Because forest tracks can be bumpy and dusty (and winter mornings can be very cold), guests with severe back problems or medical conditions should consult their doctor and consider smoother routes like Dhela or Bijrani with appropriate clothing.

8. Are you the official government website of Jim Corbett National Park?

No. Adventure Corbett Safari is an independent travel enquiry and tour assistance platform helping visitors compare safari options, stays and local arrangements. All national park entry rules, permit quotas and gate timings are governed by the Uttarakhand Forest Department / Corbett Tiger Reserve administration.
